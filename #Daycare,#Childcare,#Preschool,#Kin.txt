Title
Fun Outdoor Cup & Ball Relay Activity for Daycare Children | Coordination & Motor Skills

Description
Watch our daycare children ages 4–6 enjoy a fun and engaging outdoor cup-and-ball relay activity! 🌈🏃‍♀️🏃‍♂️

With teachers guiding and encouraging them, the children run through their colorful lanes, pick up balls, place them on cups, and race back to their teacher. This playful activity encourages focus, coordination, balance, concentration, teamwork, and gross motor skills while giving children an opportunity to enjoy friendly competition.

Our teachers support and celebrate every child’s effort and participation, creating a positive and encouraging learning environment.

Here's a deeper set of preschool (3-5) activities, built around more steps, early math and letters, and cooperative play.

**Multi-step activities**
- **Make-your-own snack:** Wash hands, spread cream cheese on a cracker, add toppings, then count the pieces before eating
- **Planting project:** Scoop soil, place a seed, water it, label the cup with a name, and check it each day
- **Treasure map:** Draw a simple map together, hide a "treasure," then follow the map step by step to find it
- **Obstacle course with instructions:** "Crawl through the tunnel, hop three times, then touch the red cone"
- **Pizza shop pretend play:** Take an order, roll the dough (playdough), add toppings, "bake," and deliver

**Early math**
- **Counting collections:** Count out 10 buttons into a muffin tin, one per cup
- **Number hunt:** Hide number cards around the room and have kids find them in order
- **Shape detectives:** Find circles, squares, and triangles around the room, then sort them into baskets
- **More or less:** Two piles of blocks, and kids decide which has more, then check by matching one to one
- **Simple patterns:** Make AB, ABB, or ABC patterns with colored blocks or beads, then let kids continue them
- **Measuring:** Compare how many blocks tall a stuffed animal is, or measure with a piece of string
- **Dice games:** Roll a die, count the dots, and move a token that many spaces

**Early letters and literacy**
- **Name building:** Spell each child's name with magnetic letters or letter stamps
- **Sound sorting:** Sort small objects or picture cards by beginning sound (/b/ for ball, bear, bus)
- **Letter sensory tray:** Trace letters in sand, salt, or shaving cream
- **Alphabet scavenger hunt:** "Find something that starts with B"
- **Rhyming games:** "What rhymes with cat?" or sing rhyming songs
- **Story sequencing:** Draw or arrange three picture cards (beginning, middle, end) after a story
- **Mark-making station:** Notepads, clipboards, and pencils for "writing" lists, menus, and signs

**Cooperative games**
- **Parachute games:** Everyone lifts together to make waves or bounce a ball without letting it drop
- **Team tower:** Build the tallest block tower possible, taking turns adding pieces
- **Group mural:** One big sheet of paper where everyone adds to the same picture
- **Pass the beanbag:** Pass to a friend while naming them, then sing or count together
- **Cooperative musical chairs:** Remove chairs but nobody gets "out," so everyone must fit on the remaining seats
- **Human knot or line-ups:** Line up by height, birthday month, or shoe color
- **Sharing station:** One set of tools (like a big puzzle) that needs teamwork to finish

**Social-emotional skills**
- **Feelings faces:** Match emotion cards and act out happy, sad, silly, or surprised
- **Turn-taking games:** Simple board games or "Hot Potato" with a soft ball
- **Helper jobs:** Line leader, snack helper, plant waterer
- **Kindness circle:** Each child says one nice thing about the friend next to them

**Tips for this age**
- Give two- or three-step directions and let kids repeat them back
- Ask open-ended questions: "What do you think will happen next?"
- Let kids help plan and clean up, which builds independence
- Keep activities to about 10-15 minutes, with a mix of active and quiet options
